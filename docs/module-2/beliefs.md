## Two visions of scientific literacy

Let's review Brian Greene's words in the last video.

> [Science] is fantastic material. This is material to die for. And yet, if it is taught in a way that we usually teach it, where we focus in so quickly on the details in order to be able to get kids to solve the equation, know the parts of the cell, balance the reaction, without a commensurate focus on the big wondrous ideas, the ideas that get us up in the morning and want us to go to work, what we do is we leave science lifeless. And what we do is we create another generation that goes out into the world and basically thinks, 'science is just a subject in school. It's best left to the scientists. It's material that I can simply leave behind when I cross the threshold of the science lab for the last time.' And that is the vital thing that we need to change.

He has outlined two ways to think about the kind of science education we need in society. Some science education scholars have termed these **Vision 1** and **Vision 2** scientific literacy.

<div class="grid cards" markdown>

-   ### Vision 1

    An education that promotes knowledge and skills that aim to produce scientists.

    That knowledge might require students to know the parts of the cell, or the skill of solving the equation and balancing the reaction.

-   ### Vision 2

    An education that promotes the interest and application of science in everyday life, regardless of whether one becomes a scientist.

    It gets students to pay attention to the products of science and enables them to evaluate those products in light of their own circumstances, such as whether they are genetically or environmentally at higher risk of contracting a disease, and then to apply steps to mitigate that risk.

</div>

While we are certainly interested in developing both Vision 1 and Vision 2 scientific literacy, in this course we offer curriculum and pedagogies that are aimed at engaging all learners who may become scientists or intelligent consumers of scientific knowledge and products, so that they will be able to use science to improve their lives.
